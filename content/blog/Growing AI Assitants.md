For the past year, I've been trying to figure out the bounds and uses for artificial intelligence. Because my first real interactions with this technology happened inside a chat window, I naturally started treating the AI like a person on the other end of a text thread. I adopted, through the pushing of AI companies, my first metaphor for understanding this new tool: AI as a personal assistant.

## AI as a Personal Assistant

The various chat bots I've interacted with are all designed to provide information about any requested topic, no matter how outlandish, in a structured back and forth conversation. As AI has access to massive stores of data, many of these interactions have yielded really useful information for me. I've often found they are a better search engine than Google itself. For example, I recently used it to plan a bike ride out in Providence, RI when I was picking up my sister in New Bedford, MA. It gave me great recommendations about various bike paths, parking and the schedule I would need to follow. It saved me hours of looking into bike paths, scrolling Reddit feeds and stressing about timing.

Extending on this metaphor, companies have now released AI agents to varying levels of success. These agents differ from the chat bots in that they actually interact in the world, buying and selling various items and performing various tasks. Although there have been some successes, the [failures](https://thetechmarketer.com/instagram-ai-chatbot-hack-2026-meta-accounts/) of these agents highlight the limits of this metaphor of understanding AI as a human assistant. To see these limitations, I've been imagining AI as a Flat Stanley who lives on a map of the world. Given the proper tools and map overlays, Flat Stanley can be incredibly adept at navigating this intricately detailed map to provide me amazing insights. But crucially, Flat Stanley does not exist in the world. He is flat, inherently connected with the map itself. 

### The Map is Not the Territory

There's this philosophical quote from Alfred Korzybski that states "the map is not the territory". The classic real world example of this statement is a GPS that directs you to drive into a lake. Because the GPS is not in the world, it has no physical understanding of the objects on the map. In the same way, AI can understand that a car is something that people drive, but it doesn't have access to the actual human experience of driving a car. All the AI can use is the data of humans talking about driving cars. Like Flat Stanley, the AI itself is not a human, and it is not in the world, so it fails in tasks that feel like common sense. This limitation led me to another metaphor to understand AI's interaction in the world: AI as a lens.

### AI as a Lens

In this metaphor, AI operates as a filter over a set of data. It reorganizes existing information in various helpful ways to expose biases and highlight trends. For example, I've been using the connected AI in the Oura app to gain insight into my sleep data. It has helped me navigate the massive amount of metrics the Oura ring collects, giving me simple recommendations tailored to my personal inclinations. Because the AI is able to quickly parse a ton of data streams simultaneously, it finds relationships that I can't see. Similar to a microscope, it reveals hidden structures in the data.

There are limitations to this power, however, much like how a physical lens can bend light to produce refraction errors. AI often notices spurious correlations in the data and generates flawed data. For example, one image recognition system used the [ruler](https://towardsdatascience.com/is-the-medias-reluctance-to-admit-ai-s-weaknesses-putting-us-at-risk-c355728e9028/) at the border of an image to determine whether an image showed cancer or not. In the generative AI arena, this refraction manifests as artifacts, creating things that don't exist, like a six-fingered person, or blending background objects.

### The Refraction Error

I've experienced these refraction errors firsthand in my various computer science projects, where an AI bot helping me code will hallucinate functions or in other projects generate unsettling images. Because AI operates statistically rather than deterministically, these refraction errors can't be solved with a simple line of code or an adjustment in the settings. For example, it's easy to assume that asking an AI to show you a certain image repeatedly will yield that exact same image every time. In reality, the AI will [slowly tweak](https://dailyai.com/2025/05/create-a-replica-of-this-image-dont-change-anything-ai-trend-takes-off/) the image with each request, culminating in a ghoulish cartoon at the end. 

Viewing AI as a lens creates the expectation that it will consistently display the same filtered data. But AI operates non-deterministically. It relies on underlying probabilities that affect the output of the system. Asking AI to retrieve a certain file doesn't actually pull that file from a hard drive; it probabilistically regenerates the shape of that file (which can be very close to the original, but crucially is not that exact file). 

### AI as a Plant

To round out my understanding of AI, I've since been thinking of it as a plant that needs nurturing and pruning to produce valuable fruit. This metaphor highlights the nature of AI to grow (or decay) over time depending on the data provided. In the chat bot interface, this directly relates to the nature of our questions. Data shows that saying [please and thank you](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/24/technology/chatgpt-alexa-please-thank-you.html) to the AI results in better responses. Although the AI is not a human and doesn't care about manners, the data vectors that are connected to the phrases "please" and "thank you" happen to be more beneficial than the data lacking those phrases. 

This metaphor creates an understanding of an AI's non-deterministic nature. A plant does not grow deterministically. Instead, it wanders and adapts over time, often ignoring any barriers in its path. 

### Paying attention to the soil

This type of interaction with AI has required a different mind set around the problems and solutions of AI—one where I have been trying to create specific environments for the AI to grow in rather than trying to impose top-down restrictions. There is plenty of evidence of these top-down restrictions failing spectacularly, from Grok going on unhinged rants after some slight tweaks, to safeguards being breached by cleverly engineered prompts requesting sensitive information within a [fictitious grandma's bedtime story](https://londondaily.com/chatgpt-s-grandma-exploit-ingenious-hack-exposes-loophole-in-ai-generates-free-software-codes). 

Instead of these restrictions, I've been thinking about how to change the soil the AI grows in. Adapting my prompts and the context window I provide to the AI works better than coding a wall to stop it from growing a certain way. I'm not sure what the ultimate interface for this kind of interactions looks like, but understanding AI through this metaphor has made me see the non-deterministic nature of AI as a benefit rather than a flaw. Because this feature of AI is inherent in the technology, I would be remiss to ignore it and try to prune it out of the blossoming forest being created.
