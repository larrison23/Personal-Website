For the past year, I've been trying to figure out exactly how artificial intelligence fits into my daily routine. Because my first real interactions with this technology happened inside a chat window, I naturally started treating the AI like a person on the other end of a text thread. I adopted, through the pushing of AI companies, my first metaphor for understanding this new tool: AI as a personal assistant.

## AI as a Personal Assistant

These systems are designed to always be ready to answer your questions, and provide you with information about any topic you ask them about. They are built as a back and forth conversation that we would normally have with other people. As AI has access to massive stores of data, many of these interactions have yielded really useful information and is often a better search engine than Google itself in finding quick answers to questions or recommendations about various activities. For example, I recently used it to plan a bike ride out in Providence, RI when I was picking up my sister in New Bedford. It gave me great recommendations about various bike paths, parking and the timing I would need to execute to be on time for my sister. It saved me hours of looking into bike paths, scrolling reddit feeds and stressing about timing.

Extending on this metaphor, companies have now released AI agents to varying levels of success. These agents actually interact in the world, buying and selling various items and performing various tasks. Although there have been some successes, the [failures](https://thetechmarketer.com/instagram-ai-chatbot-hack-2026-meta-accounts/) of these agents highlight the limits of this metaphor of understanding AI as a personal assistant, as a human in the world. Imagine a Flat Stanley who lives on a map of the world. Flat Stanley is incredibly adept at navigating this intricately detailed map and can use various overlays to look at the map in different ways (similar to how google maps can show transit lines, roads, and satellite overlays). He can tell you amazing insights about this map, give you directions and provide information as you navigate the map. But crucially, Flat Stanley does not exist in the world. He is flat, inherently connected with the map itself. 

### The Map is Not the Territory

There's this philosophical quote that states "the map is not the territory". The classic real world example of this statement is a GPS that directs you to drive into a lake. Because the GPS is not in the world, it has no physical understanding of the objects on the map. In the same way, the AI understands that a car is something that people drive, but doesn't have the human experience of driving a car. All the AI has access to is the data of humans talking about driving cars. The AI itself is not a human, and it is not in the world, so it fails in tasks that to our human conception of the world feel like common sense. This limitation led me to another metaphor to understand AI's interaction in the world: AI as a lens.

## AI as a Lens

In this metaphor, AI operates as a filter over a set of data. It reorganizes existing information in various helpful ways, finding biases, trends and insights. Through increases in the data the AI has access to, it can then map these findings to other systems and provide better and better insights. For example, AI is slowly being introduced in medical settings to [assist](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/02/02/opinion/ai-doctors-medicine.html) a doctor in image reading. It is able to find relationships that we can't see because we don't have the proper resolution into the data we are looking at. Similar to a lens, however, it can bend the light in such a way as to produce refraction errors. AI can notice spurious correlations in the data and show us insights that aren't exactly right. For example, an image recognition system just used the [ruler](https://towardsdatascience.com/is-the-medias-reluctance-to-admit-ai-s-weaknesses-putting-us-at-risk-c355728e9028/) at the border of an image to determine whether an image showed cancer or not. In the generative AI arena, this refraction manifests as artifacts, creating things that don't exist, like a six-fingered person, or blending background objects.

### The Refraction Error

We need to be careful about these errors and ensure we can elucidate the decisions AI makes about certain data. Because AI inherently operates statistically not deterministically, it can't maintain state. If you ask it to show you a certain image over and over, a state machine will show you the same image over and over, whereas if you ask AI it will [slowly tweak](https://dailyai.com/2025/05/create-a-replica-of-this-image-dont-change-anything-ai-trend-takes-off/) the image each time culminating in a ghoulish cartoon at the end. When we imagine AI as a lens, we believe it will show us the same filtered data in the same way each time. But AI operates non-deterministically. It has certain probabilities that it operates under which affect the output of the system. When we ask AI to show us a certain file, it doesn't actually retrieve that file but probabilistically regenerates the shape of that file (which can be very close to the original, but crucially is not that exact file). 

## AI as a Plant

To round out my understanding of AI, I've since been thinking of AI as a plant that needs nurturing and pruning to produce valuable fruit. This metaphor highlights the nature of AI to grow (or decay) over time depending on the data provided. In the chat bot interface, this directly relates to the nature of our questions. There's been data showing that saying [please and thank you](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/24/technology/chatgpt-alexa-please-thank-you.html) to the AI results in better responses. Although the AI is not a human and doesn't care about please's and thank you's, the data that the phrases "please" and "thank you" are connected with are more beneficial than the data that is not connected to those phrases. This metaphor creates an understanding of an AI's non-deterministic nature. A plant does not grow deterministically, but instead wanders and adapts over time, often ignoring any barriers we put up. 

### Paying attention to the soil

This type of interaction with AI requires a different mind set around the problems and solutions of AI. One where we create a specific environment for the AI to grow in rather than imposing top-down restrictions. We can see how these restrictions fail immediately, with Grok going crazy after some slight tweaks and the ability to breach AI through cleverly engineered prompts to reveal sensitive information through a [fictitious grandma's bedtime story](https://londondaily.com/chatgpt-s-grandma-exploit-ingenious-hack-exposes-loophole-in-ai-generates-free-software-codes). We can change the soil the AI grows in, through our prompts and the context window we provide to the AI, but we can't code a wall to stop the plant from growing a certain way. I'm not sure what the interface looks like when interacting in AI in this way, but understanding AI through this metaphor has made me appreciate the non-deterministic nature of AI. This feature of AI is inherent in the technology and so we would be remiss to ignore it and try to prune it out of the blossoming forest around us.
